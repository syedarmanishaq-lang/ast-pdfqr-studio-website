About this tool

A PDF to QR code generator bridges the gap between a document and the real world: one scan of a printed code opens your PDF on anyone's phone — no typing long links, no email attachments, no app to install. Restaurants put them on tables for menus, real-estate agents stick them on "for sale" signs for property brochures, teachers hand them out for worksheets, and businesses print them on packaging for manuals and warranty cards. This free PDF to QR code generator creates that bridge in seconds: paste the link to your PDF, and your browser draws a crisp, scannable QR code you can download as a PNG. Everything happens locally with JavaScript — your link is never sent to a server, there is no account to create, and there are no watermarks or usage limits.

One honest technical note: a QR code cannot hold an entire PDF file. The densest QR code stores roughly 3 kilobytes of data — a single PDF page is usually hundreds of times larger. So every legitimate "PDF to QR" workflow encodes the PDF's link, not the file itself. Anyone who scans the code opens the PDF in their browser. That is exactly what this tool does, and it does it without the part other services charge you for: uploading your document to somebody else's server.

How to turn a PDF into a QR code

  1. Put your PDF online. Upload it to Google Drive, Dropbox, OneDrive, or your own website. In Google Drive: right-click the file → Share → "Anyone with the link" → Copy link.
  2. Paste the link above. Drop the copied PDF link into the input box. The QR code renders instantly as you paste.
  3. Customize (optional). Pick the image size (1024 px for print), error-correction level (M is a good default), and colors to match your branding.
  4. Download the PNG. Click Download to save a print-ready QR code with a proper quiet zone for reliable scanning.
  5. Print or share. Add it to your menu, flyer, packaging, or sign. Anyone who scans it opens your PDF immediately.

Pro tips

Use a short, stable link. Long URLs make denser QR codes that are harder to scan from a distance. Run your Drive link through a free shortener, or better, host the PDF at a short URL on your own domain.

Print big enough. A QR code scanned from arm's length should be at least 2×2 cm; for a poster read from 2 meters away, go 10 cm or larger. Download at 1024 px so prints stay sharp.

Test with two phones. Scan the final code with both an iPhone and an Android before printing hundreds of copies. Five seconds of testing beats an expensive reprint.

Frequently asked questions

Can a QR code contain a PDF file directly?

No — a QR code holds at most about 3 kilobytes, far less than any real PDF. The standard method is encoding the PDF's web link in the QR code, so scanning it opens the PDF in a browser. That is what this free generator does.

Is this PDF to QR code generator really free?

Yes — 100% free, no sign-up, no watermarks, no usage limits. The code is generated locally in your browser, so there is nothing for us to charge for.

Do I have to upload my PDF to use this?

No. Unlike hosted PDF-to-QR services that require uploading your document to their servers, this tool never sees your PDF — you only paste its link, and the QR code is drawn on your device.

What is the difference between this and paid PDF-to-QR services?

Paid platforms (around $59 one-time) host your PDF on their servers and sell you dynamic codes with scan analytics. This tool is free and private: you host the PDF yourself and we generate a permanent static QR code for its link — no upload, no account, no tracking.

Will my QR code expire?

No. Static QR codes bake the link into the pattern permanently. Your code works forever, as long as the PDF link itself stays online.

Can I track how many people scan the code?

Static QR codes include no tracking — which is also what makes them private. If you need counts, put your PDF link through a shortener with statistics first, then generate the QR code from the shortened link.

Where should I host my PDF?

Google Drive ("Anyone with the link"), Dropbox, OneDrive, or your own website all work. For maximum control, host it on your own domain at a short, stable URL.

Is it safe for confidential documents?

Yes — safer than hosted alternatives, because nothing is ever uploaded. Only people you share the link with can open the document, and no third party ever sees it.
